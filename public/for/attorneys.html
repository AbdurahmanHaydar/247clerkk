What it costs you now

The enquiry was lost at 23:20, not at 08:30

A worried person who gets no reply messages the next firm on the search results within minutes. Your morning call-back is a formality by then.

Most enquiries are not matters

Free-advice seekers, matters outside your practice areas, disputes worth less than the fee to run them. Every one of them costs someone's morning to find out.

Conflicts surface during the consultation

The opposing party's name comes up forty minutes into a first consultation you have already given away. It should have surfaced at intake.

What your clerk establishes before you read a word

  • Practice area, against the work you actually take — so criminal defence enquiries stop landing in a commercial practice's morning.
  • Urgency and any running deadline — a 30-day CCMA clock or a looming court date changes how the morning is triaged.
  • The parties involved — captured early and checked against your open files, so a possible conflict reaches you before the consult.
  • Matter value — because a dispute worth less than the cost of running it is a favour, not an instruction.
  • Whether they want advice or representation — the free-opinion seekers identify themselves quickly when asked to book.
  • Jurisdiction — so you are not consulting on a matter that belongs in another province or forum.

Questions

Does it give legal advice?

No, deliberately. It is a clerk, not a practitioner. It answers process, fee, document and availability questions and screens the matter. The moment an enquiry calls for an opinion on the merits it says so plainly, tells the client an admitted practitioner will advise, and puts the conversation on your desk. It is built to hand over early rather than guess.

How does it help with conflict checks?

It surfaces the raw material for one. The clerk asks for the parties involved early and flags names that already appear on your open files, so a possible conflict reaches you before the consultation rather than during it. The check itself stays where it belongs — with you.

Will clients know they are talking to a clerk?

That is your call, though transparency is the sensible default for a regulated practice. Most firms have the clerk introduce itself as the firm's automated assistant. Clients rarely mind at 23:00, when the alternative is silence until Monday.

What about client confidentiality and POPIA?

Conversations stay in your account, are never sold or used to train public models, and you control retention. Because a first WhatsApp enquiry can already carry privileged detail, the clerk collects only what intake needs. We sign an operator agreement under POPIA on request.
