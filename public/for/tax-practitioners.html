What your clerk establishes before you read a word

  • Assessment or letter date — so the objection window is known before anyone opens the file.
  • Amount in dispute — the number that decides whether this is a consultation or an email reply.
  • Taxpayer type — individual, company, trust or estate — each is a different engagement and a different fee.
  • Which tax and which year — income tax, VAT, PAYE, provisional — and whether more than one period is affected.
  • Whether SARS has already been engaged — a client who has replied to SARS themselves has usually made your job harder, and you want to know now.
  • Routine work, priced and filed — standard returns get the standard answer and a booking, without reaching you at all.

Questions

Can it quote for a standard return?

Yes. You set the price list — individual returns, provisional, company, trust — and the clerk quotes it, answers what documents are needed, and books the work. It is the highest-volume and least valuable part of your inbox, and it is the part that disappears first.

Will it give tax advice?

No. It answers process, fee, document and deadline questions and establishes the facts of a dispute. Anything requiring an opinion on the assessment or on a client's position goes to a registered practitioner, and the client is told so directly.

Does it handle filing season volume?

That is the case it is built for. Volume is where an automated clerk has the clearest advantage over a person: the four hundredth pricing question of the season is answered exactly as well as the first, at 22:00, without anyone's patience being spent.
